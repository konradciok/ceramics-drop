# Print description system

## Purpose

Use this specification to turn a directly inspected print image into a short, customer-facing artwork description. The goal is not to caption every visible element. It is to select the visual characteristics that give the work its identity, translate them into a credible atmosphere or design effect, and show how the print could contribute to a room.

This system is derived from three visually verified reference pairs in this folder: `Improvise`, `Lacuna`, and `Langosta`. It governs the artwork-specific paragraph. Generic claims about paper, printing, sustainability, framing, curation, or collection membership belong in separate product-fact modules and may be used only when supported by product metadata.

## Underlying strategy

The references use a three-stage movement:

1. **Recognize the artwork:** Name the subject or abstract visual identity through a small number of high-salience cues.
2. **Interpret its effect:** Turn those cues into a restrained mood, energy, or design character that a viewer could reasonably perceive.
3. **Make it desirable in context:** Describe the role the print could play in an interior—such as a calm accent, a bold focal point, or a versatile companion to other pieces.

The copy stays specific at the visual entrance and becomes more atmospheric and lifestyle-oriented as it progresses. Desirability comes from the connection between those layers, not from a catalogue of visible details or unsupported claims about meaning.

### Product-page layers in the references

All three references place the image-responsive paragraph inside the same larger product-copy scaffold:

1. a factual opener naming the print type, artist, exclusivity, paper, and paper weight;
2. the three-sentence artwork paragraph analyzed in this specification;
3. a shared production and sustainability statement;
4. a shared list of product benefits;
5. a collection label.

Only the second layer changes substantially in relation to the image. For our products, preserve that separation: generate the artwork paragraph from vision, and add factual opener, production, care, or collection modules only from verified metadata or approved shared copy. Never make the artwork image carry factual claims it cannot prove.

### Patterns directly supported by all three examples

- The artwork paragraph is three sentences and approximately 60–75 words.
- It selects only a few dominant visual properties. Across the set these are subject or motif, palette, composition/form, and—when useful—texture or detail.
- It moves from visible evidence to atmosphere, then to an interior or styling benefit.
- It recommends plausible rooms or display roles without turning into practical decorating instructions.
- It uses polished marketing language, but visual nouns keep the claims connected to the specific image.
- It does not explain symbolism, narrate a story, or claim to know the artist’s intention.
- Technical production claims sit outside the artwork paragraph and recur as shared boilerplate.

### Reasonable interpretations of the pattern

- The writer appears to prioritize features legible at thumbnail or room-viewing distance: dominant subject, major palette, overall balance, and visual intensity. Small marks are mentioned only collectively as texture or detail.
- Abstract works need more formal description before an atmosphere feels earned; a recognizable figurative work can lead with the subject and treatment.
- Room suggestions are chosen to match the inferred visual effect: quieter work is framed as restorative or refined, while bold figurative work is framed as a focal or conversational element.
- Opposing but compatible qualities can add interest—such as playful yet polished—but only when both are credible from the image.

### What three examples cannot establish

- A mandatory house vocabulary, fixed room list, or fixed sentence syntax.
- Whether every product category should use the same length.
- Whether the title should always appear in the paragraph; it appears inconsistently in the samples.
- Whether an apparent medium or surface visible in a digital image is the actual material or production method.
- Any reliable rule for artist biography, intent, symbolism, provenance, cultural references, dimensions, or print specifications.

## Choose the visual inputs

Inspect the image before drafting. Select the smallest set of cues that makes the description unmistakably about this work:

- **Primary identity:** the main subject, motif, or type of abstraction.
- **Dominant palette:** usually two to four colour families or one defining colour contrast; include minor accents only if they materially change the impression.
- **Formal character:** one useful property such as symmetry, layering, openness, density, balance, scale, negative space, geometric order, organic flow, or directional movement.
- **Surface or mark-making:** only when clearly visible and central, described visually rather than as an unverified medium—for example, “soft-edged washes,” “grainy marks,” or “crisp contours.”
- **Overall intensity:** quiet, airy, grounded, energetic, bold, playful, graphic, or similarly defensible language.

Usually ignore:

- exhaustive object, colour, or shape inventories;
- incidental specks and minor accents;
- signatures, borders, and margins unless they are integral to the composition;
- imagined narratives, identities, settings, or symbols;
- an inferred artistic medium presented as fact.

## Move from observation to atmosphere

Use an evidence ladder. Each step must remain traceable to the one before it.

1. **Observation:** “A single saturated red figure occupies most of the pale field.”
2. **Formal effect:** “The strong silhouette gives the composition a graphic presence.”
3. **Atmosphere or interior value:** “That presence can create a lively focal point.”

Atmosphere is editorial interpretation, not fact. Phrase it as an effect the work *creates*, *suggests*, *brings*, or *can add*—never as secret knowledge about what it symbolizes or what the artist intended. Prefer one coherent emotional direction over a string of interchangeable mood words.

Keep the lifestyle claim proportionate to the visual evidence:

- muted palette + spacious or balanced composition → calm, softness, quiet refinement;
- saturated colour + strong silhouette or contrast → energy, playfulness, focal presence;
- intricate marks + layered forms → depth, tactile interest, rewarding detail;
- spare composition + ample negative space → clarity, lightness, understated impact.

These are decision aids, not automatic mappings. Reject any interpretation that does not fit the actual image.

## Typical anatomy

Write one paragraph of two to three sentences; three is the reference-set norm.

### Sentence 1 — visual hook and immediate effect

Identify the artwork through its subject or most distinctive formal relationship. Include one or two concrete visual cues, then introduce the dominant mood or character.

**Inputs:** `[title if useful]`, `[subject or abstract relationship]`, `[dominant palette/form]`, `[immediate effect]`.

### Sentence 2 — develop the visual logic into an interior role

Use another major property—composition, scale, texture, movement, contrast, or restraint—to explain what the print could add to a space. Optionally name one to three plausible rooms or a compatible decor context.

**Inputs:** `[supporting visual trait]`, `[focal/accent/grounding role]`, `[room or styling context]`, `[credible atmosphere]`.

### Sentence 3 — close with a second reason to choose it

Add one non-redundant benefit: visible detail, textural interest, display versatility, contrast with neutral surroundings, or ability to work alone or in a grouping. End on the work’s overall design character, not a generic sales superlative.

**Inputs:** `[secondary visible strength]`, `[display option or pairing]`, `[concise design payoff]`.

Do not force all three sentences if the work supports only two distinct ideas. A compact, well-grounded description is better than padded copy.

## Tone and language

- Polished, warm, visually literate, and design-conscious.
- Specific enough to identify the image; abstract enough to leave the viewer interpretive room.
- Moderate marketing intensity: inviting and aspirational without hype, urgency, or guarantees.
- Mostly medium-to-long sentences, varied by a shorter closing phrase or clause.
- Active or sensory verbs connect appearance to effect.
- Adjectives work in selective pairs; avoid stacks of near-synonyms.
- Room and styling language is suggestive, not prescriptive.
- No first person, artist voice, exclamation marks, or calls to action.

Useful grammatical patterns include:

- artwork/subject + active verb + visual feature + effect;
- visual feature + creates/brings/adds + spatial or emotional value;
- participial clause for the transition from appearance to consequence;
- optional contrast pair when the image supports both qualities.

Vary the syntax and vocabulary between products. These are rhetorical tools, not sentences to reuse.

## Reusable semantic template

Use the placeholders as decisions, not blanks that must all be filled:

> `[Optional title or subject-led opening]` `[neutral active verb]` `[primary subject or abstract relationship]` through `[one or two dominant visual cues]`, `[restrained immediate effect]`. `[Supporting formal or surface trait]` `[creates/brings/adds]` `[interior role or atmosphere]` in `[optional plausible rooms or decor context]`. `[Optional secondary visible strength or display versatility]` `[offers/completes/supports]` `[specific final design payoff]`.

The final prose must not expose placeholders, repeat this syntax mechanically, or rely on stock phrases from the references.

## Optional branches

### Abstract or non-representational print

Lead with the relationship among palette, shapes, marks, or layers. Use composition and movement to earn the atmosphere. Do not invent a subject from ambiguous forms.

**Possible progression:** palette/form interaction → balance, rhythm, or movement → mood and room role → texture/detail or grouping versatility.

### Figurative, object, animal, or botanical print

Lead with the recognizable subject, then qualify how it is rendered: simplified, detailed, oversized, delicate, graphic, loosely drawn, and so on. Use colour, silhouette, pose, scale, or negative space to support the emotional and interior effect.

**Possible progression:** subject + treatment → visual presence/personality → focal or accent role → pairing or display flexibility.

### Minimal or strongly graphic print

Let contrast, scale, silhouette, spacing, or repetition do the work. Keep the prose comparably spare and avoid adding “texture” merely to fill a slot.

### Dense or highly textural print

Select one organizing feature before discussing detail. Describe the experience of layered or close-looking interest without itemizing every mark or claiming a physical texture that cannot be verified.

## Supported facts and prohibited inventions

The image alone can support descriptions of visible subject, colour, form, composition, apparent mark quality, spatial balance, and a clearly signalled editorial mood. Supplied metadata can additionally support title, artist, verified medium, materials, process, dimensions, edition, provenance, and other product facts.

Never infer or invent:

- artist intention or the “meaning” of the work;
- symbolism, cultural significance, or narrative backstory;
- physical medium, paper, ink, finish, or printing process from appearance;
- tactile qualities as physical product facts (describe *visible* texture instead);
- artist biography, provenance, exclusivity, awards, or collection status;
- dimensions, orientation options, framing, sustainability, durability, or archival quality;
- universal emotional promises such as what every viewer will feel.

If verified metadata is included, weave in only what improves the customer-facing description. Keep generic production information outside the artwork paragraph unless the requested format explicitly combines them.

### Optional full-description assembly

When the task calls for a full product description and verified factual modules are supplied, assemble them in this order:

1. `[Optional factual identity line: print type + title/artist + verified product facts]`
2. `[Original artwork-specific paragraph generated with this system]`
3. `[Optional approved production, sustainability, care, or fulfilment copy]`
4. `[Optional verified benefit bullets or collection label]`

Do not synthesize missing modules. Omitting an unsupported layer is correct.

## Final quality check

Before delivering, verify:

1. A reader could distinguish this artwork from the other products based on the opening visual cues.
2. Every interpretive or lifestyle claim has a visible basis.
3. The paragraph selects rather than inventories.
4. The progression moves from artwork to effect to interior value without abrupt leaps.
5. No unsupported factual claim has entered through confident wording.
6. The language is original and does not reproduce distinctive phrases from any reference.
7. The prose sounds like finished product copy, not an image analysis or a filled-in template.
